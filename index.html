HotCup

A companion for airline crew — on duty and in between.

Hot Cup reads a crew member's published monthly roster and helps them use the time around it: the hours in a layover city, and the free days at home. Built for pilots and cabin crew.

In development · not yet published

What it does

Reads the roster

The monthly roster becomes a calendar: duties, flight legs, layovers and free days, shown with the airline's own codes rather than re-interpreted. Fields the parser could not read confidently are flagged for the crew member to confirm — never quietly assumed.

Answers duty-limit questions

Crew describe their situation in plain language and get an answer drawn from official aviation regulation documents, with the source passages shown and a separate verification pass. It is a decision-support tool; the final authority remains the official sources and the operator.

Suggests what to do with the time

For a layover, it works out the genuinely free hours — after transfer, sleep and getting ready — and suggests places that fit, with real travel times and a guaranteed time back at the hotel. For the home base, it keeps a shared calendar of what is on in the city that month.

Where the information comes from

Every recommendation carries its source. Nothing is shown that cannot be traced back to a page a person can open and check — if a source cannot be named, the entry is not published at all.

  • Google Maps Platform — places and real travel times around a layover hotel, shown live with attribution.
  • Tripadvisor (planned) — ratings and things to do near the crew hotel, displayed with Tripadvisor attribution and bubble ratings and linking back to Tripadvisor. Fetched live rather than stored.
  • Public event pages — venues, ticketing providers and municipal culture calendars, each entry linking to the page it came from.
  • Official regulation documents — used only for duty-limit answers, with the relevant passage quoted.

When something is not known, the app says so. An event with no announced time shows time not stated rather than a plausible guess, and a month that has not been checked yet is shown as unchecked rather than empty.

Privacy

Crew rosters contain personal data that has nothing to do with planning an afternoon. Personnel numbers, passport and document numbers, colleagues' names, vaccination and medical dates and hotel phone numbers are stripped before the roster is parsed — if the cleaning step fails, the upload is refused rather than stored.

Roster images are processed in memory and not kept. Profile photos stay on the device. Raw roster text never appears in logs.

Hot Cup is an independent project and is not affiliated with any airline.
